## Photograph selection

Most entries use Bullseye's fired medium-frit spread: a dense region at the left and progressively more scattered grains toward the right. The official catalog describes these samples as fired on 3 mm clear glass, with selected white styles photographed over a black-glass base. The complete photograph is bundled without a compositional crop, at up to 600 pixels on its longest side.

For 16 transparent tints, the nearly colorless fired spread cannot show enough visible hue, so the catalog uses the official medium-frit product's cast-wedge photograph. Only a reviewed rectangle within the front glass face is sampled, above the black backdrop boundary and away from the top face, bright edges, or white background. These entries explicitly record `imageKind: Cast medium-frit wedge photograph`; they are not mislabeled as spread photographs. No physical wedge dimensions or calibrated depth-to-color curve were provided with these photos.

Medieval Green (001242) and Light Mineral Green (001247) currently use four-grain fired comparison photographs. Only the top-right tile explicitly labeled `-0002` is sampled. Their cold-grain dish photo, the other three grain sizes, printed labels, and margins are excluded. All source images and special sampling rectangles were visually reviewed before inclusion.

## Material sampling and background rejection

Sampling operates on the full-resolution sRGB source using 40 narrow vertical bands across each reviewed rectangle. Visible material pixels are selected before averaging in linear RGB. White gaps between blue grains cannot become a white sample; black gaps around white grains cannot become a gray sample. Measurements are actual retained material averages, not generated blends.

Chromatic source pixels and resulting means must have CIELAB chroma `C* = sqrt(a*² + b*²) >= 10`. Neutral source pixels and means must have `C* < 10`. These are deliberate library selection thresholds for visibly colored versus neutral samples, not universal perceptual boundaries. Neutral photographs also use the product's reviewed lightness interval: ordinary gray samples exclude near-white base areas, black samples use `L* <= 20`, and white or pale neutral glass over black is confined to a dense left rectangle with a specified high-lightness interval. Warm gray and cream products retain only their eligible neutral material. French Vanilla is sampled from visibly yellow material in its dense left region, avoiding dark gaps and blue fringe reflections.

A band needs at least 5% eligible material and at least 32 original material pixels. A mean that crosses the selected chroma boundary is discarded. No missing colors are invented to fill a range. Each sample marker points to eligible source material near its measured mean in Lab. If that source grain loses visible color when downscaled, the marker moves a few source pixels to eligible material that remains visible in the encoded preview; the measured mean is unchanged. Its geometrical band center and material coverage are recorded separately so the marker cannot simply fall in a white gap. All masks, thresholds, and rectangles are retained in the product record.

## Lightness and the estimated thickness guide

Raw appearance samples are ordered by descending CIELAB L* (light to dark). Their stored `darkness` uses the same absolute scale for every product: `clamp(100 − L*, 0, 100)`. Thus a photographed black sample stays near 100 rather than being relabeled 0 because it happened to be the lightest black sample in a narrow product range. Full-color matching continues to use all three Lab coordinates.

Each product also records an actual `thicknessReference` color from the darkest eligible sampled band in the left quarter of its reviewed dense-material region. This reference is independent of array ordering and is not an inferred physical depth. The extension's estimated thickness guide compares a transparent sample to that dense reference. Opalescent colors use their full-color reference rather than claiming a linear transparency relationship. User reference dimensions are an artist-supplied guide, not a Bullseye calibration of these photographs.

## Color profiles and reproducibility

Embedded source ICC profiles are converted to sRGB using Pillow/LittleCMS. Untagged storefront JPEGs are explicitly interpreted as sRGB; their original capture profile is unknown. Bullseye's archive documentation describes Adobe RGB originals, which does not establish the profile of an untagged storefront derivative. Every bundled JPEG embeds an sRGB ICC profile. No automatic white balance, contrast, saturation, or palette correction is applied.

Records preserve the source URL and SHA-256, metadata SHA-256, bundled-image SHA-256, dimensions and profile handling, review status, image kind, selected rectangle, and retrieval timestamp. Variant IDs, sizes, SKUs, availability, and prices come from the public product JSON. Prices are USD integer cents and are snapshots; the store provides live prices and availability when shopping.

Rebuild with `python scripts/build-catalog.py` after installing `requirements-dev.txt`. A previously downloaded and reviewed source cache may be supplied with `--source-cache PATH`. Review changed photos before distributing a rebuilt catalog: review flags remain true only when source-image hashes match the reviewed hashes in the seed. `--limit N` is a development fixture that replaces the output with that subset.
